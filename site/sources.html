Secondary sources

  • Bass, Warren. 2003. “Support Any Friend.” Cfr.Org. Council on Foreign Relations. June 2, 2003. The current source analyses the foreign policies of US President John F. Kennedy towards the Middle East countries during the Cold War period, specifically in regard to his diplomatic ties with Israel and Egypt. In the source, Bass analyzes American attempts to boost their regional influence, build diplomatic ties with the Arab states, and foster military ties with Israel. The significance of this source lies in the fact that it was produced by a historian and published by Oxford University Press.
  • “Gregory Brew.” n.d. Accessed November 17, 2026. This source discusses the elements that led to the decision of the Eisenhower administration to remove Iranian Prime Minister Mohammed Mossadegh from power in 1953. Brew posits that U.S. policymakers were concerned about the collapse of Iran economically and politically since it would mean that the country would fall under communist rule if the oil problem was not solved. This source is significant since it is a peer-reviewed article that is based on information from declassified government papers. I have utilized this source to highlight the significance of economic and petroleum interests in American policymaking.
  • Yaqub, Salim. 2003. “Contesting Arabism: The Eisenhower Doctrine and the Arab Middle East, 1956-1959.” Academic talk, November 24. This source discusses the Eisenhower Doctrine and US containment of Soviet and Arab nationalist expansion in the Middle East after the Suez Crisis. According to Yaqub, the US backed conservative Arab regimes to curb the increasing influence of the Egyptian President, Gamal Abdel Nasser. This source is credible since the author uses diplomatic documents and other scholarly sources to back up his historical discussion. I have used this source to highlight how the US was motivated by Cold War considerations and other factors in making its commitment to safeguard national sovereignty.
  • Hahn, Peter L. 2006. “Securing the Middle East: The Eisenhower Doctrine Of 1957.” Presidential Studies Quarterly 36 (1): 38–47. This source looks into the Eisenhower Doctrine and its impact on the increase in American political and military presence in the Middle East region. Hahn analyzes American economic aid to Jordan, the struggle against Soviet influence in Syria, and the military presence in Lebanon in 1958. The credibility of the source can be assured due to the fact that it was published in an academic journal. This source was chosen to show how American commitment to security and stability in the region was defined by strategic interests and competition.
  • Lahav, Pnina. 2015. “The SUEZ CRISIS of 1956 and ITS AFTERMATH: A COMPARATIVE STUDY of CONSTITUTIONS, USE of FORCE, DIPLOMACY and INTERNATIONAL RELATIONS PNINA LAHAV .” This source deals with the political, diplomatic and constitutional elements that lead to the 1956 Suez Crisis. Lahav discusses why Britain, France and Israel were motivated to undertake military actions against Egypt, and how President Eisenhower was able to use diplomacy and economics to prevent them from doing so. This source is reliable since it is a scholarly article on international law with facts backed up by historical sources and academic literature. I used this source to show how the opposition of the U.S. to military acts was also a manifestation of international laws and diplomacy.
  • Khan, Mohd Naseem. 2001. “The US Policy Towards the Persian Gulf: Continuity and Change.” Ciaotest.Cc.Columbia.Edu. May 2001. The source looks at the evolution of U.S. foreign policy towards the Persian Gulf from the end of World War II to 2001. The issues discussed in the source include America’s interest in oil, Soviet containment, the 1953 coup in Iran, the Carter doctrine, the Gulf War, and the U.S. military presence in the area. The source can be deemed reliable since it is a journal article that is backed up by official records and academic studies. The use of this source was intended to show how American pledges for security and independence of the region were dictated by economic and strategic considerations.
  • Office of the Historian. 2019. “The Suez Crisis, 1956.” State.Gov. 2019. The document offers a historical account of the events surrounding the Suez Crisis of 1956. The account includes the nationalization of the Suez Canal by Egypt, the invasion of the canal by Britain, France, and Israel, and the diplomatic efforts of the US to resolve the crisis. The document illustrates how the Eisenhower administration supported the anti-colonial policies of the period without allowing for any possible Soviet involvement in the region. The source is reliable because it has been written by the Office of the Historian at the US Department of State.
